# Incident Handler's Journal — Ransomware Incident Analysis

## Overview

This activity is part of the Google Cybersecurity Certificate program. The objective was to practice documenting a cybersecurity incident using an incident handler's journal and the **5 W's framework (Who, What, When, Where, and Why)**.

The activity involved reviewing a simulated ransomware attack against a small healthcare clinic in the United States.

Through this exercise, I practiced identifying the attack method, documenting the incident, understanding the attacker's motivation, and considering potential security weaknesses.

## Scenario

A small healthcare clinic experienced a ransomware attack that disrupted its business operations.

The attackers sent phishing emails containing malicious attachments to employees. After an employee downloaded and executed a malicious attachment, the attackers gained access to the organization's systems and deployed ransomware.

The ransomware encrypted critical files, preventing the clinic from accessing important patient data and continuing normal operations.

The attackers left a ransom note demanding payment in exchange for a decryption key.

## Incident Analysis — The 5 W's

| Question | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Who?** | An organized group of malicious hackers. |
| **What?** | A ransomware attack initiated through phishing emails containing malicious attachments. |
| **When?** | Tuesday at 9:00 AM. |
| **Where?** | A small healthcare clinic in the United States. |
| **Why?** | The attackers sought financial gain by encrypting the organization's files and demanding a ransom payment. |

## Attack Sequence

Based on the scenario, the attack followed these steps:

1. **Phishing Email:** The attackers sent targeted phishing emails to the clinic's employees.
2. **Malicious Attachment:** An employee downloaded and executed a malicious attachment.
3. **Unauthorized Access:** The attackers gained access to the organization's systems.
4. **Ransomware Deployment:** The attackers deployed ransomware that encrypted critical files.
5. **Ransom Demand:** The attackers left a ransom note requesting payment for a decryption key.
6. **Business Disruption:** The clinic could no longer access critical patient data and had to shut down affected systems.

## Tools Used

- **Incident Handler's Journal:** Used to document the incident details and organize findings.
- **5 W's Framework:** Used to summarize the key information about the incident.

## Additional Investigation Questions

After reviewing the incident, I identified two questions that could help evaluate the organization's security posture.

**1. Does the organization have an effective email filtering mechanism?**

Since the incident began with a phishing email, I would investigate whether the organization uses email security filtering to identify suspicious attachments and malicious messages.

**2. Does the organization provide regular security awareness training?**

Employees may not always recognize phishing attempts. Regular security awareness training could help employees identify suspicious emails and reduce the likelihood of similar attacks.

## Security Recommendations

Based on the scenario, several security measures could help reduce the risk of future ransomware incidents:

- **Email Security:** Implement or improve email filtering to detect phishing messages and malicious attachments.
- **Security Awareness Training:** Educate employees on recognizing and reporting phishing attempts.
- **Regular Backups:** Maintain secure, regularly tested backups to support recovery from ransomware attacks.
- **Access Control:** Apply the principle of least privilege to limit unauthorized access and reduce the potential impact of compromised accounts.

These are proposed security improvements based on the scenario, not controls that were tested or implemented during the activity.

## Key Takeaways

Through this activity, I learned that:

1. **Incident documentation is important.** Recording accurate incident details helps security teams understand what happened and supports future investigations.
2. **Phishing can lead to serious security incidents.** A single malicious attachment can provide an entry point for attackers.
3. **The 5 W's framework helps organize information.** It provides a structured way to document and communicate incident details.
4. **Security awareness and email protection are important.** Preventive security controls can help reduce the likelihood of successful phishing attacks.
